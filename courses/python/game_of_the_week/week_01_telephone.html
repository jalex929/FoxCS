Game of the Week — Lesson 1: Telephone

How to Play

Sit in a line or circle. Your teacher whispers a short message to the first person. That person whispers exactly what they heard to the next person, and so on down the line — no repeating, no asking to check. The last person says out loud what they heard. Then compare it to the original message.

What This Game Is Actually Doing

Every step is a chance for the message to change

Telephone is really a game about information transfer — passing something from one person (or one part of a system) to the next. In games, that happens constantly: a player's button press has to "transfer" into the game doing the right thing, and one player's move has to transfer into what the next player sees and reacts to.

The message almost never survives the whole line unchanged, and that's the real lesson. Each whisper is a breakdown point — a specific place where the message can get lost, misheard, or changed a little before it moves on. One small breakdown near the start of the line usually causes a much bigger difference by the end. Designers have to think hard about where a game's own breakdown points are, since players will find them.
