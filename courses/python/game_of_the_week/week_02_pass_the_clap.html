Game of the Week — Lesson 2: Pass the Clap

How to Play

Stand in a circle. One person starts by turning to face the person next to them, making eye contact, and clapping together at the exact same moment, like passing a single clap along rather than two separate ones. That person immediately turns to the next person and passes it the same way, all the way around the circle. Only one clap should ever be moving at a time. If it stalls, or two people clap with two different neighbors at once, that is a break, so just start it again. Once a round feels smooth, try speeding it up, or let a player reverse the clap back the way it came instead of passing it forward.

What This Game Is Actually Doing

Only one clap moves at a time

Pass the Clap only works if exactly one clap is moving around the circle at a time. Every player has to wait their turn, receive the clap, and only then pass it on. That is turn-taking, one of the oldest structural decisions a designer can make. In Mario Party, only one player rolls the dice and moves at a time. Everyone else has to watch, wait, and plan their own next move, which feels completely different from a game where everyone acts at once.

The other thing Pass the Clap plays with is pacing, how fast or slow the action moves. A slow, careful round feels nothing like a fast one where the clap is flying around the circle, or one that suddenly reverses direction. Tetris does the same thing on purpose: blocks fall slowly at first, then speed up the longer you play. That is the exact same idea as speeding up the clap, using pace itself to raise the pressure.
