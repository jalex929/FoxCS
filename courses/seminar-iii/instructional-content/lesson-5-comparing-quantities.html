Comparing Quantities

This week's big question: what two quantities am I comparing? Ratios, rates, and proportions are all different ways of answering it.

What Does a Ratio Compare?

A ratio compares two quantities. Some ratios compare a part to another part — 5 forwards to 7 defenders. Others compare a part to a whole — forwards to the entire 12-player team. These aren't the same statement: 5 forwards out of 12 total players is 5/12 of the team, not 5/7.

Writing and Simplifying Ratios

The same ratio can be written three ways: 3 to 2, 3:2, or 3/2. Order matters — 3:2 and 2:3 describe different comparisons. Simplify a ratio the same way you'd simplify a fraction, using the greatest common factor: 18:12 simplifies to 3:2. The relationship being compared doesn't change, just the numbers used to describe it.

Equivalent Ratios

Scale a ratio up or down by multiplying or dividing both quantities by the same factor.

Common mistake: if a recipe ratio needs more sugar, some students add the same amount to every ingredient instead of multiplying. Equivalent ratios come from multiplying or dividing, not adding.

What Is a Rate?

A rate is a ratio that compares quantities with different units — miles to hours, dollars to items. Every rate is a ratio, but not every ratio is a rate. Keep the units attached as you work; they're part of the answer, not an afterthought.

Unit Rates

A unit rate divides so the second quantity becomes exactly 1 — dollars per one item, miles per one hour.

$104 for 8 hours of work: 104 ÷ 8 = $13 per hour.
A unit rate isn't automatically “bigger number ÷ smaller number.” It's whichever quantity the question calls the reference — the “per one” unit.

Comparing Rates: Best Buy

To compare two offers, calculate each one's unit rate, then compare those directly.

A higher total price doesn't automatically mean a worse deal — the unit rate decides value, not the sticker price.

Proportions

A proportion is a statement that two ratios (or rates) are equal — for example, 4/5 = x/30.

Solving Proportions

Two paths to the same answer: cross multiply, or find a unit rate and scale. Either way, the proportion has to be set up with matching quantities in matching positions first — cross multiplication only gives a correct answer when that's true.

Solve 4/5 = x/30: cross multiply: 4×30 = 5×x, so 120 = 5x, x = 24.

Unit Conversion

A conversion factor is just a ratio, arranged so the unit you don't want cancels out. Flip it, and it cancels the wrong thing — watch for a result that's obviously the wrong size.

Error Detective

Knowledge — “I don't remember how to set up a proportion.”
Fix: review the setup rule — matching quantities in matching positions.
Process — correct setup, wrong cross multiplication
The proportion was set up correctly but the cross-multiplication steps went wrong. Fix: review the steps and retry.
Execution — correct setup and cross multiplication, arithmetic slip
Everything about the method was right; the final division had a small mistake. Fix: check the calculation and verify with estimation.
Comprehension — comparing forwards to defenders instead of forwards to the whole team
The question asked for a part-to-whole comparison; the student compared part-to-part instead. Fix: reread the question and identify exactly what's being compared.
Strategy — comparing total prices instead of unit rates
The reasoning wasn't wrong, just inefficient and easy to misread. Fix: convert to a unit rate before comparing.

Quick Reference

Ratio: a comparison of two quantities.
Rate: a ratio comparing different units.
Unit rate: a rate where the second quantity equals one.
Proportion: a statement that two ratios or rates are equal.
Solving a proportion: cross multiply, or find the unit rate and scale.
