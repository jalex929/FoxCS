Numbers That Make Sense

This week's big question: Does my answer make sense?

Numbers Tell a Story

A positive or negative sign is not just decoration. It tells you something real about a situation.

  • Money: +$50 means gaining $50. −$50 means owing or losing $50.
  • Temperature: 8°F is eight degrees above zero. −8°F is eight degrees below zero.
  • Elevation: +100 ft could mean 100 feet above sea level. −100 ft could mean 100 feet below sea level.
  • Change: +12 could mean an increase of 12. −12 could mean a decrease of 12.

Before you calculate anything, ask what the sign actually means in the situation.

Which Number Is Greater?

On a number line, numbers farther to the right are greater.

−8 < −2 < 0 < 3 < 10

Every positive number is greater than every negative number. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

Common Mistake Assuming a negative number with a bigger digit is bigger. It's the opposite: −12 is less than −5, not greater. Think about debt: would you rather owe $5 or owe $12? A balance of −$5 is better (greater) than a balance of −$12.

Absolute value is the distance a number is from zero. |−8| = 8 and |8| = 8, because both are 8 units from zero. Absolute value describes distance, so it is never negative. It does not mean "make the number positive." A temperature of −4°F does not become 4°F just because its absolute value is 4. It means −4°F is four degrees away from zero.

Working With Positive and Negative Numbers

Addition, same direction: −5 + (−3). Start at −5, move 3 more in the negative direction. Result: −8.
Addition, opposite directions: −5 + 8. Start at −5, move 8 in the positive direction. Result: 3.
Subtraction: 8 − (−3) = 11. Subtracting a negative removes a negative quantity, which increases the result. If a $3 loss is removed, your financial position improves by $3.

Multiplication and division signs:

Same signs → positive Different signs → negative

This shortcut applies to multiplication and division only. It does not transfer to addition and subtraction.

Which Operation Do I Need?

  • Addition: combined, total, gained, increased by.
  • Subtraction: difference, decreased, remaining, change between values.
  • Multiplication: equal groups, a repeated amount, rate times quantity.
  • Division: split equally, amount per group, how many groups, or finding a rate from a total and a quantity.

Keywords are clues, not guarantees. Always ask: what quantities do I have, what am I trying to find, and how are the quantities related?

What Happens First? (Order of Operations)

  1. P: Parentheses (or other grouping symbols)
  2. E: Exponents
  3. M/D: Multiplication and Division, left to right
  4. A/S: Addition and Subtraction, left to right
6 + 3 × 4  →  multiply first: 3 × 4 = 12  →  then add: 6 + 12 = 18 (not 36)
Common Misconception Multiplication does not always happen before division. They have equal priority, worked left to right. 20 ÷ 5 × 2 is worked as 20 ÷ 5 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. It is not 20 ÷ (5 × 2) = 2.

The same is true for addition and subtraction: equal priority, left to right. 12 − 5 + 3 is 7 + 3 = 10, not 12 minus (5+3).

Estimate Before You Trust

Estimating gives you a rough target so you can catch mistakes.

49 × 21  →  round to 50 × 20  →  ≈ 1,000

The exact answer should land near 1,000. If a calculator shows 10,290, that should immediately look wrong. Estimating is not random guessing. It is rounding to numbers you can work with easily, then reasoning from there.

Using a Calculator Well

Calculators are useful for lengthy arithmetic, decimals, and checking your work. They cannot decide which operation a situation needs, what a question is actually asking, or whether your answer is reasonable.

Watch Out A calculator only evaluates what you typed in. If you meant to calculate 52 × 18 but typed 52 × 81, the calculator will correctly answer the wrong question. Estimate first, and check what you entered.

Does My Answer Make Sense?

Before trusting an answer, check it against five things:

OperationDid I use the correct operation for this situation?
SizeIs the answer approximately the size I expected?
SignShould the answer be positive, negative, or zero?
UnitDid I answer in the correct unit (dollars, degrees, items)?
ContextDoes this answer actually make sense in the situation?

Learn From the Error

Not every wrong answer means the same thing. Use the five error types from Week 2:

Knowledge: "I don't remember the rule for multiplying negative numbers." Response: review the concept.
Process: using the wrong order of operations. Response: review the steps and retry.
Execution: setting up 42 × 6 correctly but making an arithmetic mistake. Response: check the calculation.
Comprehension: calculating the final amount when the question asked for the amount of change. Response: reread the question.
Strategy: doing a long exact calculation when estimating would have been faster. Response: consider a more efficient approach.
