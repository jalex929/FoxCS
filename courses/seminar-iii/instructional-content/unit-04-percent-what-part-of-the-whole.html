Seminar III · Unit 04 · Percent

Percent: What Part of the Whole?

This week's big question: What does this percent represent?

What Does Percent Mean?

The word percent comes from the idea "per hundred." Percent means out of 100.

25% = 25/100 = 0.25 = 1/4

A percent is not a new type of number. It is another way to represent a quantity you already know how to work with as a fraction or a decimal.

Fraction, Decimal, or Percent?

Fraction, decimal, and percent are three representations of the same quantity. The representation changes. The quantity does not.

1/2 = 0.5 = 50%1/4 = 0.25 = 25%3/4 = 0.75 = 75%

Decimal to percent: multiply by 100. 0.42 × 100 = 42, so 0.42 = 42%.

Percent to decimal: divide by 100. 18 ÷ 100 = 0.18, so 18% = 0.18.

Percent to fraction: write the percent over 100, then simplify. 40% = 40/100 = 2/5.

Fraction to percent: convert to a decimal first, then to a percent. 3/5 → 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6 → 60%.

Benchmarks can be combined. For example, 15% = 10% + 5%. If 10% of $60 is $6, then 5% of $60 is $3, so 15% of $60 is $9.

Also remember: 100% means the entire amount. 50% means half. 200% means twice the original quantity. A percent is not required to stay between 0% and 100%.

Which Percent Problem Is This?

Almost every percent problem is one of three structures. Identify which quantity is missing before calculating.

Find the PartFind the PercentFind the Whole
  • Find the Part: the whole and the percent are known. Example: what is 30% of 80?
  • Find the Percent: the part and the whole are known. Example: 18 is what percent of 24?
  • Find the Whole: the part and the percent are known, but the whole is not. Example: 15 is 25% of what number?

Finding a Percent of a Number

Convert the percent to a decimal, then multiply by the whole.

30% of 80: 0.30 × 80 = 24

Since 30% is less than 100%, the answer should be smaller than 80. This is a built-in reasonableness check.

Benchmark reasoning can be faster. Find 25% of 60: since 25% = 1/4, calculate 60 ÷ 4 = 15.

Finding What Percent

Divide the part by the whole, then convert the decimal result to a percent.

18 out of 24 learners completed an assignment. Part ÷ whole: 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 = 75%

Identify the part and the whole before dividing. The part goes on top.

Finding the Whole

Try benchmark reasoning first, then division as a backup.

$15 is 25% of what amount? 25% = 1/4, so 15 × 4 = $60
18 is 30% of what number? 18 ÷ 0.30 = 60. Check: 30% of 60 is 18.

One of the most important skills this week is identifying what amount represents 100%, the whole. Ask: what is the complete starting amount? For a discount, it is the original price. For a test score, it is the total possible points. For percent change, it is the original value.

Discounts

A discount reduces an original price. Calculate the discount, then subtract it from the original price.

A $70 jacket is 20% off. Discount: 0.20 × 70 = $14. Sale price: 70 − 14 = $56.
Watch the Question "How much is the discount?" and "what is the sale price?" have different answers ($14 versus $56) from the same calculation. Reread the question before answering.

Mental estimate: an item costs about $48 and is 25% off. One-fourth of $48 is about $12, so the sale price should be around $36. If a calculator returns $4.80, something is wrong.

Tax

Sales tax increases the cost. Calculate the tax, then add it to the original price.

A $40 purchase has 10% tax. Tax: 0.10 × 40 = $4. Total: 40 + 4 = $44.
Watch the Question "How much tax is charged?" ($4) is different from "what is the final cost?" ($44). Tax always increases the amount owed, it never decreases it.

Tips

Tips are a practical use of mental percent strategies.

A $36 meal, 20% tip: 10% of $36 = $3.60, so 20% is double that, $7.20.

For messier totals, round first. A $47.80 meal rounds to about $50. 10% of $50 is $5, so 20% is about $10. A tip around $9 to $10 is reasonable, and an exact calculation can follow if needed.

Percent Increase and Decrease

Percent can describe how much a value changes relative to where it started.

  1. Identify the original value and the new value.
  2. Find the change: new value minus original value.
  3. Divide the change by the original value.
  4. Convert the decimal result to a percent.
A value rises from 50 to 60. Change: 60 − 50 = 10. Compare to the original: 10 ÷ 50 = 0.20 = 20% increase.
A price drops from $80 to $60. Change: 80 − 60 = 20. Compare to the original: 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25 = 25% decrease.
Common Mistake The denominator should always be the original value, not the new value. A value rising from 40 to 50 has a change of 10. The correct setup is 10 ÷ 40, not 10 ÷ 50.

Percent change is relative, not absolute. Two workers each get a $5 raise. Worker A started at $10/hour: 5 ÷ 10 = 50% increase. Worker B started at $25/hour: 5 ÷ 25 = 20% increase. The same dollar amount produces very different percent changes.

Common Mistake A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the starting amount. A $100 item increased 20% becomes $120. Decreasing $120 by 20% removes $24, leaving $96, not $100. The second 20% is calculated from a different whole.

Does My Answer Make Sense?

Continue using Operation, Size, Sign, Unit, Context, with a few percent-specific questions added.

Percent SizeIf the percent is under 100%, should the part be smaller than the whole?
DirectionIs this a discount (should decrease) or an increase (should grow)?
Which AmountDid I answer with the percent amount, or the final amount?
Estimate CheckIs my exact answer close to my estimate?

Example: a learner calculates 25% of 80 = 320. This should immediately look wrong. 25% is one-fourth, and one-fourth of 80 must be smaller than 80. In fact, 80 ÷ 4 = 20.

Error Detective

Not every wrong answer means the same thing. Use the five error types from earlier weeks.

Knowledge: treating 20% as the whole number 20 instead of 0.20. Response: review the meaning of percent.
Process: correctly finding a discount amount but forgetting to subtract it from the original price. Response: identify the final step the problem requires.
Execution: correctly setting up 0.20 × 70 but entering 0.20 × 700 into the calculator. Response: check the entered values against an estimate.
Comprehension: the question asks how much tax is charged, and the learner reports the total cost after tax. Response: restate what the question is asking before answering.
Strategy: using a long decimal calculation to find 50% of 86 instead of recognizing that 50% means half. Response: choose the more efficient approach.
