Finding and Doing Your Work in Moodle

This course lives on a website called Moodle. This page walks you through exactly how to use it.

What Is Moodle?

Moodle is the website where you will find your Seminar III work. Your teacher posts readings, practice sheets, and assignments there, organized by unit. You will check it regularly, the same way you would check any other class website.

Logging In

  1. Go to the course website. Your teacher will give you the exact web address. [Web address to be provided.]
  2. Enter your username and password. Your teacher will give you your login information. Keep it somewhere you can find it again.
  3. Save the page. Bookmark it, or add it to your phone or computer's home screen, so you do not have to search for it every time.
If You Get Stuck If the page will not load, or your username and password do not work, tell your teacher right away instead of guessing. A small login problem is easy to fix quickly; a login problem that goes unreported for a week is not.

Finding Your Course

After you log in, you will land on your Dashboard. This is a summary page listing every course you are enrolled in. Look for FoxCS: Seminar III and click on it. That takes you into the actual course, where your units live.

How the Course Is Organized: Units, Not Weeks

Inside the course, work is organized into units, numbered starting at Orientation. You are in Orientation right now.

  • Units are not tied to a specific calendar week. Lesson 1 is not "week 1 of the year." It is simply the first real content unit, after this orientation unit.
  • Units go in order. Each one builds on the skills from the one before it, so work through them in sequence rather than skipping around.
  • You will move through units at your own pace, within the goals your teacher sets. If a unit takes you a little longer or a little less time than someone else, that is expected.

Opening Your Work

Inside a unit, you will see a list of items. Click on one to open it. Most items are one of two things:

A reading or handoutOpens right in your browser. Read it the same way you would read any web page.
A practice sheetA page you work through, either on screen or printed, depending on what your teacher asks for that day.

If an item does not open, try clicking it again once. If it still does not open, tell your teacher. Do not assume it is broken and skip it.

Staying on Track

Every Monday, you will do a short check-in: check your current grades, identify anything missing or incomplete, and set a specific goal for the period. This is the same routine described in the course overview, and Moodle is where you will look to answer the first two questions.

Before you ask "what should I be working on," check the current unit in Moodle first. The answer is almost always there.

Getting Help

Asking for assistance the right way is itself a skill this course teaches, not a sign you are behind. If something in Moodle is not working, or you are not sure what a unit is asking you to do, ask your teacher. Be specific: which unit, which item, and what exactly is confusing or not working.
