Looking Back Across a Whole Quarter

This week's big question: What have I learned about how I learn?

Why This Week Is Different

Unit 08 does not teach a new skill. Weeks 3 through 8 built the first full Quarter 1 sequence: Numbers & Operations, Fractions & Decimals, Percent, Ratios/Rates/Proportions, Variables & Expressions, and Equations. This week asks you to look back across all six at once, instead of one skill at a time.

The goal is not a test for its own sake. The goal is to leave Quarter 1 with an honest, evidence-based answer about how you learn: a skill you have mastered, a skill still developing, a skill that should be a priority in Quarter 2, and the error pattern that shows up most often in your own work.

Recognizing a Skill Without Being Told

Skills taught in separate weeks are often practiced in isolation. You might do well on a percent question during Unit 04, then fail to recognize a percent situation buried inside a Unit 08 mixed problem, simply because the skill was never asked for outside its own week.

Example: A recipe calls for 3 eggs for every 2 cups of flour. If a baker uses 9 eggs, how many cups of flour are needed?

This problem never says "this is a ratio problem." You have to recognize the comparison yourself. That recognition, not the calculation, is the real skill this week adds on top of Weeks 3 through 8. It is also closer to how problems appear on the ACT and in real situations, where nothing is pre-sorted by topic.

The Quarter 1 Growth Check

The Growth Check is this week's cumulative assessment: 28 to 32 questions spanning every Quarter 1 domain, some clearly labeled by topic and some mixed and unlabeled on purpose. It is informative, not punitive, in the same spirit as the Unit 01 baseline.

  1. Numbers & operations, fractions & decimals, percent: direct retrieval, similar to what Unit 01 tested.
  2. Ratios/rates/proportions, expressions, equations: new mastery, since these were not part of the Unit 01 baseline.
  3. Mixed, unlabeled application: requires recognizing which skill applies before solving anything.
Before You Begin Some questions will not announce which skill they require. Attempting an unfamiliar-looking question still gives useful information. Use the Unit 01 problem-solving routine throughout: STOP, FIND, CONNECT, TRY, CHECK.

Reading Your Results

A raw score, such as 21 out of 31, does not tell you what to do next. Use the same three-part language established in Unit 01 instead.

Current StrengthsSkills you now demonstrate consistently, including any measurable growth since Unit 01.
Developing SkillsSkills you partially demonstrate.
Priority SkillsSkills that need continued instruction or review heading into Quarter 2.

Where a Unit 01 baseline question has a direct match on the Growth Check, compare the two results directly. Where a skill was not part of the baseline (percent, ratios/rates, expressions, equations), report it as new mastery rather than growth from a prior score, since there was no prior score to grow from.

Synthesizing a Quarter of Errors

You already know the five error categories from Unit 01. This week, apply them across an entire quarter of evidence instead of a single problem. Review your error-analysis records, exit tickets, and check results from Weeks 2 through 8 to answer each question with real evidence, not a guess.

Knowledge: Did the same "I don't remember this" error appear in more than one week?
Process: Did errors happen most often while following multi-step procedures, such as two-step equations or proportion setups?
Execution: Did errors happen most often through small arithmetic or sign mistakes, even when the approach was correct?
Comprehension: Did errors happen most often from misreading what a question asked, such as confusing a part-to-whole ratio with a part-to-part ratio?
Strategy: Did errors happen most often from choosing an inefficient approach, such as long calculation when estimation would have worked?

Whichever category appears most often across the quarter, not just in one week, is your most frequent error pattern. That pattern tells you what to prioritize going into Quarter 2.

How to Pick a Priority Skill

A priority skill is not simply "whatever felt hardest." Use evidence from three places before deciding:

  1. Growth Check accuracy by domain: which domain had the lowest accuracy?
  2. Confidence ratings from the Day 1 Skill Map activity: did you rate a skill low, and did the Growth Check confirm it?
  3. Error pattern: does one error type keep showing up specifically inside one domain?

A priority skill supported by all three kinds of evidence is a stronger, more specific target than a guess. This is the same evidence you will use to complete the Unit 08 Quarter 1 Reflection.
