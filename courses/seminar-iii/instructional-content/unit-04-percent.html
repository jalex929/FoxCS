What Part of the Whole?

This week's big idea: percent means out of 100.

Percent Means Out of 100

The word percent comes from the idea of "per hundred." A percent is just another way to represent a quantity you already know how to work with as a fraction or a decimal.

25% = 25/100 = 0.25 = 1/4

Percent is not a new kind of number. It is a new way of writing a number you already understand.

Converting Between Forms

  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100. 0.42 × 100 = 42, so 0.42 = 42%.
  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100. 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35, so 35% = 0.35.
  • Percent to fraction: write it over 100, then simplify. 40% = 40/100 = 2/5.
  • Fraction to percent: convert to a decimal first, then to a percent. 3/5 = 0.6 = 60%.
Common Misconception Writing 20% as 20 instead of 0.20. A percent under 100% should always convert to a decimal less than 1.

10% of a number: divide by 10. 1% of a number: divide by 100. Other percents can be built from these: 15% = 10% + 5%.

Watch Out Percentages are not limited to 0 to 100%. 200% means twice the original amount. 100% means the entire amount.

Three Percent Questions

Almost every percent problem is one of three structures. Identifying which one you have matters more than memorizing a formula.

Find the PartWhat is 30% of 80? The whole and percent are known.
Find the Percent24 is what percent of 80? The part and whole are known.
Find the Whole24 is 30% of what number? The part and percent are known.
Find the part: percent (as a decimal) × whole. 30% of 80: 0.30 × 80 = 24.
Find the percent: part ÷ whole, then convert to percent. 18 out of 24: 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 = 75%.
Find the whole: part ÷ percent (as a decimal). 18 is 30% of what number? 18 ÷ 0.30 = 60.

Discounts and Sale Price

A discount reduces an original price. A jacket costs $70 and is 20% off.

Discount: 0.20 × 70 = $14. Sale price: 70 − 14 = $56.
Common Comprehension Error A learner correctly finds the $14 discount, then reports $14 as the sale price. Discount amount and sale price are two different answers to two different questions.

Sales Tax

Tax increases the amount owed. A purchase costs $40 before 10% tax.

Tax: 0.10 × 40 = $4. Total: 40 + 4 = $44.

Discount gets subtracted. Tax gets added. Mixing those two up is a common mistake, not a sign that the percent calculation itself was wrong.

Estimating Tips

A meal costs $36. To estimate a 20% tip: find 10% first, then double it.

10% of $36 = $3.60. Double it for 20%: $7.20.

This same 10%-then-adjust strategy works for almost any tip percentage without needing exact decimal multiplication.

Percent Increase and Decrease

Percent change compares how much a value changed to where it started, not to where it ended up.

A value rises from 50 to 60. Change: 60 − 50 = 10. Compare to the original: 10 ÷ 50 = 0.20 = 20% increase.
Common Misconception A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the original amount, because the second 20% is calculated from a new, different whole.

Does My Answer Make Sense?

Continue the Quarter 1 reasonableness routine, with a few percent-specific checks added:

  • If the percent is under 100%, the part should be smaller than the whole.
  • A discount should make the price go down. Tax should make it go up.
  • Percent change should always be compared to the original value, not the new one.
  • Did I answer with the percent amount, or the final total amount?
