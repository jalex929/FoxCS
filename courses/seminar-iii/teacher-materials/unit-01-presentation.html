The Routine

The Five Questions

One routine, used all year, for math and non-math problems alike.

Introduce one question at a time — the next five slides.

Question 1

What information do I have?

numbersfactsdefinitions labelsdiagramsgraphs tablesinstructionscontext
Say: don't calculate yet — first figure out what's actually available.

Question 2

What am I trying to determine?

"I need to find __________."

Have students say the sentence out loud before they touch a calculation — it stops blind calculating without a goal.

Question 3

What skill or information could help?

additionpercentagesfractions ratiosan equationa graph a formulaa definition
Students don't need the whole solution yet — just a lead worth following.

Question 4

What strategy can I try?

write an equationdraw a diagrammake a table estimateeliminate choiceswork backward try a simpler example
The important word is try. It doesn't need to be guaranteed to work.

Question 5

Does my answer make sense?

  • Too large? Too small?
  • Positive or negative?
  • Does the unit make sense?
  • Did I answer what was actually asked?
This question matters more and more as the quarter goes on — don't rush past it.

Teacher Think-Aloud

"Watch me think through one, out loud."

A student earns $15 per hour and works 6 hours. How much before taxes?
Model it live, narrating all five questions as you go — STOP ($15/hr, 6 hrs) → FIND (total earned) → CONNECT (rate × hours) → TRY (15 × 6) → CHECK (should land between $75 and $100 — $90 does).

Day 2

Why Did I Get It Wrong?

Introduce the five error types · analyze sample wrong answers · students classify 8–10 scenarios

Set-Up

"Wrong" Doesn't Tell Us Enough

Three students answer the same problem incorrectly — for three different reasons.

  • Student A — doesn't know the rule
  • Student B — knows the rule, calculates wrong
  • Student C — solves correctly, answers a different question

"Do these students need the same help?"

The Framework

The Five Error Types

An incorrect answer doesn't automatically mean "I don't know how to do this."

Error Type 1

Knowledge Error

"There's something I need to know that I don't currently know or remember."

Example: doesn't remember how negative numbers behave during multiplication. — Next step: review the concept, study a worked example.

Error Type 2

Process Error

"I had the right general idea, but something went wrong in my steps."

Example: uses the correct formula but substitutes values into the wrong places. — Next step: compare with a correct example, write out intermediate steps.

Error Type 3

Execution Error

"I knew how to solve it, but I made a mistake while doing the work."

Example: copying 36 as 63; dropping a negative sign. — Next step: slow down, check work against an estimate.

Error Type 4

Comprehension Error

"I didn't correctly understand what the question was asking or telling me."

Example: finds the radius when asked for diameter; overlooks the word "NOT." — Next step: reread, underline the actual task.

Error Type 5

Strategy Error

"I understood the problem, but the approach I chose wasn't the best way to solve it."

Example: solving algebraically for several minutes when substituting answer choices would be faster. — Next step: ask "is there an easier way?"

Recap

Error Types at a Glance

TypeMain Question
KnowledgeDid I know the information or skill I needed?
ProcessDid I correctly follow the steps?
ExecutionDid I make a smaller mistake doing something I knew how to do?
ComprehensionDid I understand the question correctly?
StrategyDid I choose an effective approach?
Errors can overlap — the goal isn't a perfect label, it's answering "what should I do differently next time?"

Practice

Error Detective

Let's classify these together. Click each answer choice to check yours.

Error Detective · 1 of 5

6 + 3 × 4 = ?
Student answers 36 (6+3=9, then 9×4=36)
Process Error

Correct calculations, wrong order of operations. Review order of operations and retry.

Error Detective · 2 of 5

A $80 jacket is discounted 25%.
Student correctly finds the discount is $20 and answers $20.
Comprehension Error

Calculated the discount, not the final price. Identify exactly what's being asked before answering.

Error Detective · 3 of 5

−7 + 12 = ?
Student: "I don't remember what I'm supposed to do when one number is negative."
Knowledge Error

Review adding positive and negative numbers.

Error Detective · 4 of 5

48 ÷ 6 = ?
Student correctly picks division, writes 48 ÷ 6 = 6.
Execution Error

Right operation, wrong arithmetic. Check by multiplying the answer back out.

Error Detective · 5 of 5

A multiple-choice problem could be solved fast by testing the four choices. Student instead builds a long multi-step equation, spends six minutes, doesn't finish. Reasoning is valid throughout.
Strategy Error

Look for a faster method before starting a long calculation.
