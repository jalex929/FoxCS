Confidence Check-In Set

The baseline reflection ("01.14") asks students to rate confidence on these six representative questions, spanning easy-to-hard and multiple domains: B01 (order of operations), B09 (percent of a number), B13 (proportion), B18 (two-step equation), B20 (perimeter word problem), B23 (reverse reasoning). Cross-reference a student's confidence rating against whether they actually got the question right — Correct+Confident suggests a real strength; Incorrect+Confident often flags a genuine misconception worth a direct conversation, not just more practice.

Teacher Discussion Prompts (Day 4)

  • Show two students' results with the same total score but different missed questions. Ask: do these two students need the same kind of help? Why or why not?
  • Ask a student who missed a question with high confidence to explain their reasoning out loud — this is usually where a real misconception (not just a careless slip) surfaces.
  • For a student who missed several questions in one domain (e.g., all three Percent questions), ask whether the pattern feels like "I don't know this" (Knowledge) or "I know it but something goes wrong" (Process) — the follow-up support is different for each.
  • Normalize a low score explicitly: this is the first data point of the year, not a grade, and Quarter 1's instruction is about to directly target whatever shows up as a Priority Skill here.

Results Summary Template

When reviewing results with a student (individually or as a class pattern), use this language rather than a bare score:

  • Current Strengths: domains/skills answered correctly and consistently (e.g., no misses across all questions in that domain).
  • Developing Skills: domains with a mix of correct and incorrect, or correct-but-low-confidence answers.
  • Priority Skills: domains with multiple misses, especially where confidence was also low or the same error type repeats.

Quarter 1's instructional sequence (Units 02-08: Numbers & Operations, Fractions & Decimals, Percent, Ratios/Rates/Proportions, Variables & Expressions, Equations) already covers every domain tested here — a student's Priority Skills should visibly connect to the units still ahead, not feel disconnected from what's coming next.
