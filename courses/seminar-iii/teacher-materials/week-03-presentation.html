Day 1

Positive, Negative & Absolute Value

What does the sign mean? Which number is greater? How far from zero?

Numbers Tell a Story

A positive or negative sign isn't decoration — it tells you something real.

+$50 gained−$50 owed 8°F above zero−8°F below +100 ft elevation−100 ft below sea level

Before you calculate anything, ask what the sign actually means in the situation.

Which Number Is Greater?

Farther right on the number line means greater.

Every positive number is greater than every negative number. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

A Trap Worth Knowing

Is −12 greater than −5?

No — on the number line, −12 sits farther left.

Think about debt: would you rather owe $5, or owe $12? A balance of −$5 is better (greater) than a balance of −$12.

Absolute Value

The distance a number is from zero.

|−8| = 8 and |8| = 8, because both are 8 units from zero. Absolute value is never negative — it doesn't mean "make the number positive." A temperature of −4°F doesn't become 4°F just because its absolute value is 4; it means −4°F is four degrees away from zero.

Day 2

Operations & Order

Adding and subtracting with negatives · choosing the right operation · order of operations

Addition With Negatives

Think of it as movement on the number line.

  • −5 + (−3): start at −5, move 3 more negative → −8
  • −5 + 8: start at −5, move 8 positive → 3

Subtraction With Negatives

8 − (−3) = 11

Subtracting a negative removes a negative quantity, which increases the result. Think of it in context: if a $3 loss is removed from your situation, your position improves by $3.

Let's Work One Together

A bank account has $45. A $62 charge is processed. What's the new balance?

  • STOP: $45 balance, $62 charge.
  • FIND: the new balance.
  • CONNECT: a charge decreases the balance.
  • TRY: 45 − 62 = −17.
  • CHECK: a $62 charge on a $45 balance should go below zero — it does.

Answer: −$17

Multiplying & Dividing Negatives

Same Signs → Positive

Different signs → negative. This applies to multiplication and division only.

It doesn't transfer to addition/subtraction — that's a different rule, and mixing them up is a common trap.

Which Operation Do I Need?

Keywords are clues, not rules.

  1. What quantities do I have?
  2. What am I trying to find?
  3. How are the quantities related?

"Total means add" and "each means multiply" are helpful clues — but the situation decides, not the word.

What Happens First?

6 + 3 × 4
  • Wrong: 6+3=9, then 9×4=36
  • Right: 3×4=12, then 6+12=18

A Trap Worth Knowing

Does multiplication always come before division?

20 ÷ 5 × 2

No — multiplication and division have equal priority. Work left to right: 20÷5=4, then 4×2=8 (not 2). Same for addition/subtraction: 12−5+3 → 7+3 = 10, not 12−(5+3).

Day 3

Estimation, Calculators & Reasonableness

About what should the answer be — before you trust the exact one?

Estimate Before You Trust

49 × 21  →  round to 50 × 20  →  ≈ 1,000

If a calculator shows 10,290, that should immediately look wrong. Estimating isn't random guessing — it's rounding to numbers you can work with easily, then reasoning from there.

Which Answer Could Be Right?

49 × 21 = ?
A. 102.9   B. 1,029   C. 10,290   D. 102,900

50 × 20 ≈ 1,000, so only B is reasonable. You can eliminate three choices without calculating exactly — that's the whole point of estimating first.

A Calculator Isn't a Correctness Check

You meant 52 × 18, but you typed 52 × 81.

The calculator correctly answers the wrong calculation. It can't tell you that. Estimate first, then double-check what you actually entered.
