Percent

Percent means out of 100. Once you can see it as a fraction and a decimal wearing a different outfit, discounts, tax, tips, and percent change are all the same handful of moves.

Session A

What Percent Means, and Finding Part / Percent / Whole

Percent Means Out of 100

25 out of 100 squares are shaded — that's 25%. The same amount is 25/100, 0.25, or 1/4. All four are the same quantity, just written differently.

Fraction, Decimal, or Percent?

The representation changes. The quantity doesn't.

10% = 1/1025% = 1/450% = 1/275% = 3/4

You'll move between all three constantly, so these benchmarks are worth knowing cold.

Percent Shortcuts Worth Knowing

You can find most percentages in your head by combining a few benchmarks: 1%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%.

15% of a number = 10% of it, plus 5% of it (half of the 10%).

Which Percent Problem Is This?

Every percent problem is one of three types. Before calculating, name which one you have.

Finding a Percent of a Number

30% of 80: write 30% as 0.30, then multiply — 0.30 × 80 = 24.

Finding What Percent One Number Is of Another

Divide the part by the whole, then convert to a percent.

18 out of 24 correct: 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 = 75%.

Finding the Whole

Try benchmark reasoning first. Otherwise, divide the part by the decimal percent.

15 is 20% of what number? 15 ÷ 0.20 = 75. Check: 20% of 75 is 15. ✓

Before We Move On

Part, percent, or whole? Identify it — then solve.

Session B

Percent in Money, and Percent Change

Discounts and Tax: Label What You're Finding

An $80 jacket, 25% off: discount = 0.25×80 = $20. Sale price = 80−20 = $60.
A $50 purchase, 8% tax: tax = 0.08×50 = $4. Final cost = 50+4 = $54.

A discount reduces the price. Tax increases it. Keep the amount and the final price straight — they're never the same number.

Tips

You don't need exact restaurant-etiquette rules — you need a fast, useful estimate. Find 10% by moving the decimal point one place, double it for 20%, or add half of the 10% for 15%.

Percent Increase and Decrease

original → new → difference → percent change
A price rises from 40 to 50: change = 10. 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25 = 25% increase.

Percent change always divides by the original value — using the new value instead is a common trap.

Does My Answer Make Sense?

  • Is the part smaller than the whole when the percent is under 100%?
  • Should a discount decrease the price? Should tax increase it?
  • Did I use the original value for percent change?
  • Did I answer with the percent amount, or the final amount — whichever was actually asked for?
