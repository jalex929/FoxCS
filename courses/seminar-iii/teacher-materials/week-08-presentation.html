Seminar III · Week 8 · Foundations

Equations

What does the unknown represent, and what operation will undo what's happening to it?

Two teaching sessions, not four separate lessons.

Session A

One-Step and Two-Step Equations

What Is an Equation?

Think of it as a balance.

Solve using the inverse operation of whatever is currently applied to the variable — model one example each of addition, subtraction, multiplication, division.

Common Misconception

Do I only need to change one side?

No — whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other, or the equation is no longer balanced.

Common Misconception

x + 7 = 12 — multiply both sides?

No — 7 is being added, so subtraction undoes it. Use the inverse of what's actually there.

Two-Step Equations

Undo in reverse order.

3x + 5 = 20
Undo addition/subtraction first (subtract 5), then undo multiplication/division (divide by 3) — not the order the equation was built in.

Common Misconception

Does −2x + 9 = 1 need a different method?

No — same steps. Dividing by a negative just needs the Week 3 sign rules.

Exit Ticket — Session A

  1. Solve a two-step equation
  2. Solve one with a negative coefficient
  3. Identify the correct first step for a given equation

Session B

Combine/Distribute First, Then Check & Apply

Before You Solve

Combine like terms and distribute first.

Combining 2x + 3x into 5x doesn't change what the equation means. Distributing is technically optional in some cases, but it's more reliable, especially with extra terms outside the parentheses.

Common Misconception

Does checking a solution mean re-solving it?

No — substitute the solution back into the original equation and confirm both sides are equal.

Common Misconception

Once x is found, is the word problem finished?

Not yet — the value has to be interpreted in context, with the right units, before it's a real final answer.

Common Misconception

If the solution is a fraction or decimal, was there a mistake?

Not necessarily — check the solution for reasonableness in context, don't reject it just for not being a whole number.
