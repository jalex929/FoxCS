Ratios and Rates

What Is a Ratio?

Part-to-part, or part-to-whole?

students to computerswins to lossesrecipe ingredients
Same relationship, three ways to write it: 3 to 2, 3:2, 3/2.

Common Misconception

Does 3:2 mean the same as 2:3?

No — order tells you which quantity is being compared to which.

Common Misconception

Does simplifying 18:12 to 3:2 change what's being compared?

No — same relationship, different scale.

Common Misconception

5 forwards : 7 defenders — are forwards 5/7 of the team?

No. The team has 12 players total, so forwards are 5/12 of the team — not 5/7. Part-to-part isn't part-to-whole.

Equivalent Ratios & Unit Rates

Scale by multiplying, not adding.

Wrong: "add 2 cups of sugar, so add 2 cups of everything else." Right: multiply or divide every quantity by the same factor.

Common Misconception

Does a higher price always mean a worse deal?

No — the unit rate determines value, not the total price.

Session B

Proportions and Unit Conversion

Setting Up a Proportion

Matching quantities in matching positions.

Introduce cross multiplication and the unit-rate method as two paths to the same answer — then always check the solution.

Common Misconception

Can any two fractions in a proportion be cross multiplied?

Only if the proportion was set up with matching quantities in matching positions.

Common Misconception

Is a unit rate always the larger number divided by the smaller?

No — divide so the reference quantity becomes 1, based on what the question asks "per," not which number is bigger.

Unit Conversion

A conversion factor is just a ratio.

The trick is arranging it so the unwanted unit cancels — and checking multi-step conversions with estimation.

Common Misconception

Does it matter which unit goes on top of a conversion factor?

Yes — flip it and the unwanted unit doesn't cancel. The result comes out wrong, often obviously unreasonable.

Activity — Convert and Compare

Price per ounce vs. price per pound — convert to a common unit first.

  • Why does the unit need to match before comparing?
  • What happens if the conversion factor is flipped?
  • How can estimation catch an unreasonable conversion?
