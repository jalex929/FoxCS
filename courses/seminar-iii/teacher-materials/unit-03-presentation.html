Common Misconception

Is 1/8 greater than 1/4, because 8 > 4?

No — more pieces means each piece is smaller.

1/8 < 1/4. Use benchmark fractions and a visual to make this concrete.

Common Misconception

Does simplifying make a fraction smaller?

6/8 = 3/4
The representation changes. The quantity doesn't.

Adding Fractions

Same-size pieces first.

Wrong: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5. The pieces must be rewritten with a common denominator first.
Like denominators: add numerators directly. Unlike denominators: find a common denominator first.

Multiplying Fractions

No common denominator needed.

2/3 × 3/5  →  multiply numerators, multiply denominators
A common misconception is hunting for a common denominator here — that's an addition rule, not a multiplication one.

Dividing Fractions

"Keep, change, flip" — but ask why first.

Before the shortcut, ask: what does this division actually mean? Watch for flipping the wrong fraction or flipping both.

Common Misconception

Must a fraction be less than 1?

No — improper fractions are legitimate values, not "wrong" answers to fix.

Session B

Decimals & Choosing a Representation

Decimal place value and operations · fractions and decimals as the same number, different form

Common Misconception

Is 0.475 greater than 0.8, because 475 > 8?

No — place value decides, not digit count.

0.800 > 0.475. Line up decimal points to compare.

Common Misconception

Right digits, wrong decimal placement.

2.4 × 3 — a student answers 72
Estimate first: 2 × 3 ≈ 6. An answer of 72 should immediately look wrong.
