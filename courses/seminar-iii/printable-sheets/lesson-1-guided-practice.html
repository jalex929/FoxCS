Using the Five-Question Routine

Worked Example: Do I have enough time?

You have 3 chores left, taking about 30, 45, and 20 minutes. Practice starts in 2 hours.

  1. What information do I have? Three chores: 30, 45, 20 minutes. Two hours (120 minutes) until practice.
  2. What am I trying to determine? Whether the total chore time fits inside 120 minutes.
  3. What skill could help? Addition.
  4. What strategy can I try? Add the three times, then compare to 120.
  5. Does my answer make sense? 30 + 45 + 20 = 95. 95 is less than 120, so yes, there is enough time, with about 25 minutes to spare.

Now try these. Write out all five questions for each:

  1. A school trip costs $18 per learner. 24 learners are going. The school has a $350 budget. Is the budget enough?
  2. A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of sugar per batch. You want to make 2 batches. How much sugar do you need?
  3. You need to be at work by 5:00 PM. It takes 25 minutes to get ready and 15 minutes to drive there. What time should you start getting ready?

Classifying the Error

Worked Example

A learner solves 9 − 4 + 2 and answers 3, working it as: 4 + 2 = 6, then 9 − 6 = 3.

  1. The learner added before subtracting, which changed the order of operations. Addition and subtraction have equal priority and should be worked left to right: 9 − 4 = 5, then 5 + 2 = 7.
  2. The learner understood the numbers and the operations involved, but performed the steps in the wrong order.
  3. Best classification: Process Error. Useful next step: rework the problem left to right, one step at a time.

Now try these. For each, name the most likely error type and explain your reasoning:

  1. A question asks how many more books Group A read than Group B. A learner reports the combined total read by both groups instead.
  2. Asked to find 10% of 150, a learner says, "I don't remember how to find a percent of a number."
  3. A learner correctly sets up 6 × 7, then writes the answer as 48.
  4. On a multiple-choice problem, a learner spends 8 minutes solving a complicated equation by hand when testing the four answer choices would take under a minute.
  5. A learner adds −6 + 9 and answers −15.
