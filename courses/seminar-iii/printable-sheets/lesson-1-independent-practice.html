Seminar III · Lesson 1

Independent Practice

Show your work where it applies.

Apply the Five-Question Routine

For each scenario, write out all five questions and your answer to each.

  1. A jacket costs $64 and is on sale for 20% off. You have $55. Can you afford it?
  2. Your teacher assigns a project with 4 parts, due in 6 days. You can work on it about 45 minutes a day, and the whole project takes about 4 hours. Is that enough time?
  3. Station A sells gas for $3.29 a gallon. Station B sells it for $3.15 a gallon but is farther out of your way. For a 10-gallon fill-up, which station saves you more money?

Classify the Error

For each scenario, name the most likely error type (Knowledge, Process, Execution, Comprehension, or Strategy) and explain your reasoning in one sentence.

  1. Asked to compare −12 and −5, a learner says, "I don't know which negative number is bigger."
  2. Asked what the median of a data set is, a learner says, "I'm not sure what that word means."
  3. Solving 2 × (3 + 4), a learner multiplies 2 × 3 = 6 first, then adds 4, getting 10.
  4. Solving a multi-step equation, a learner subtracts before dividing when division should have come first.
  5. A learner correctly sets up 15% of 60 as 0.15 × 60, then types it into a calculator as 0.15 × 6, getting 0.9.
  6. Copying an answer from scratch work onto the final response, a learner writes 72 instead of 27.
  7. A word problem asks how much change is left from a $20 bill after a purchase. A learner answers with the cost of the purchase itself.
  8. Reading a bar graph, a learner reports the value for the wrong category because they misread the label.
  9. A learner redoes a long division problem three times by hand instead of estimating first to check whether the answer is even reasonable.
  10. Given four answer choices, a learner sets up and solves a full equation instead of testing which choice actually works.
