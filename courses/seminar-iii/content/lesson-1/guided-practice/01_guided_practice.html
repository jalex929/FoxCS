Reference: The Five-Question Routine

Keep this open while you work. You do not need to memorize it.

  1. STOP — What do I know?
  2. FIND — What am I solving for?
  3. CONNECT — What tool fits?
  4. TRY — Do the math.
  5. CHECK — Does it make sense?

Worked Example: Do I have enough time?

You have 3 chores left, taking about 30, 45, and 20 minutes. Practice starts in 2 hours.

  1. STOP: What information do I have? Three chores: 30, 45, 20 minutes. Two hours (120 minutes) until practice.
  2. FIND: What am I actually solving for? Whether the total chore time fits inside the available time.
  3. CONNECT: What tool applies? Addition, then comparison.
  4. TRY: Do the math. 30 + 45 + 20 = 95 minutes.
  5. CHECK: Does the answer make sense? 95 minutes is less than 120 minutes, so yes, there's enough time, with 25 minutes to spare.

Classifying the Error

Worked Example

A learner solves 9 − 4 + 2 and answers 3, working it as: 4 + 2 = 6, then 9 − 6 = 3.

  1. The learner added before subtracting, which changed the order of operations. Addition and subtraction have equal priority and should be done left to right: 9 - 4 = 5, then 5 + 2 = 7.
  2. The idea (combine the numbers using the operations shown) wasn't wrong, but the order the steps were done in was wrong.
  3. This is a Process Error: the right general idea, with the steps done out of order.
