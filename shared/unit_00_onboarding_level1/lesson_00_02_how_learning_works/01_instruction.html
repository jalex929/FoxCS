0.2 — How Learning Works

What you'll take away:
  • I can describe what mastery actually means in this class, and why it's not the same as getting everything right the first time.
  • I can explain the Reinforce, Core, and Extend levels and how they help me get unstuck or push further.

Here's something worth saying out loud before you write a single line of code or design a single pixel: you are not expected to get things right on the first try. Nobody in this class is.

Learning something genuinely new, whether that's Python, JavaScript, HTML and CSS, or Unity and C#, follows a predictable shape. You try something. It doesn't quite work. You figure out why. You try again, a little differently. Eventually it clicks. That loop is not a sign you're behind. It's what learning actually looks like, for everyone, every time.

What Mastery Actually Means

Mastery isn't perfect first-attempt performance, memorizing everything, or being fast. Mastery is being able to solve a new problem using what you've practiced, even if you have to revise your first attempt to get there.

Debugging, revising, and trying something a second or third way are part of mastery, not evidence that you failed. A finished project that went through several rough drafts shows just as much mastery as one that happened to work the first time, sometimes more.

Reinforce, Core, and Extend

Most practice in this class is organized into three levels. You'll see this pattern show up again and again, so it's worth understanding now.

Reinforce
For when a concept still feels shaky. Simpler versions of the same idea, more scaffolding, more repetition.
Core
The main target for everyone. If you can do the Core work, you've learned what the lesson set out to teach.
Extend
For when Core feels solid and you want a harder version, a twist, or a chance to go further.

You're allowed to move between these. Starting at Reinforce because something isn't clicking yet isn't a lesser path, it's the right path for that moment. Jumping to Extend because Core felt easy isn't showing off, it's using your time well.

How This Actually Plays Out

You won't usually be told "go do Reinforce now." Most of the time, you'll notice for yourself: this doesn't make sense yet, or, this feels too easy, I want more. Learning to notice that about your own understanding is itself a skill, and it's one this class is built to help you practice.

What This Means Day to Day

  1. You'll get instruction and examples first, before you're asked to do something on your own.
  2. Practice comes in small pieces with fast feedback, not one big test at the end of everything.
  3. Projects let you apply what you've learned to something bigger, with room to make real choices.
  4. Mastery checks are where you show what you actually know, on your own, in your own words or code.

None of this works if you skip the struggle part and copy someone else's answer, or have an AI tool do it for you. That's covered fully in an upcoming lesson (Academic Integrity), but the short version is: the struggle is the point. It's literally where the learning happens.
